################ SCENES ################

By Tone & Mood:
Atmospheric Scene – Focuses on setting a mood or tone (e.g., eerie, tranquil, tense).
Suspenseful Scene – Builds tension and anticipation.
Horror Scene – Designed to frighten or disturb.
Comedic Scene – Intended to be humorous or lighthearted.
Dramatic Scene – High emotional intensity, often with conflict.
Melancholic Scene – Evokes sadness or nostalgia.
Romantic Scene – Centers on love or intimacy.
Whimsical/Fantastical Scene – Playful, dreamlike, or surreal.

By Narrative Function:
Exposition Scene – Introduces key information or backstory.
Climactic Scene – The peak of tension or turning point.
Resolution Scene – Wraps up a storyline.
Flashback Scene – Reveals past events.
Foreshadowing Scene – Hints at future events.
Montage Scene – A sequence of condensed events (common in films).
Training/Progress Scene – Shows a character learning or improving.
Revelation Scene – A major secret or truth is uncovered.

By Interaction & Dynamics:
Dialogue-Driven Scene – Heavy on conversation.
Action Scene – Physical movement, fights, chases.
Silent Scene – Relies on visuals, expressions, or music.
Confrontation Scene – Two or more characters clash.
Reunion Scene – Characters meet after time apart.
Betrayal Scene – A character is deceived or double-crossed.
Seduction Scene – Romantic or manipulative persuasion.

By Setting & Visual Style:
Battle Scene – Large-scale combat (common in war/epic genres).
Chase Scene – Fast-paced pursuit.
Heist Scene – A carefully planned theft or mission.
Courtroom Scene – Legal drama, arguments, verdicts.
Party Scene – Social gathering with multiple interactions.
Isolated Scene – A character is alone (e.g., survival, introspection).
Dream/Hallucination Scene – Unreal or distorted reality.
